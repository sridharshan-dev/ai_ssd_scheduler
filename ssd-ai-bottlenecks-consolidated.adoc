== The Physical Ceiling (Read This First)

Modern GPUs (e.g., NVIDIA H100) move data through their memory
subsystems at rates exceeding *3 TB/s*, while conventional storage
delivers *10-40 GB/s* under ideal conditions -- a *30-100x* gap
`[SOURCED]`. A single fast NVMe drive practically tops out around
*7-14 GB/s* depending on PCIe generation and drive count `[SOURCED]`.
PCIe Gen 5 provides roughly 64 GB/s (x4 duplex) versus GPU HBM3e at
3.2-4.8 TB/s `[PLAUSIBLE]`.

IMPORTANT: No firmware trick, cache, or in-storage compute closes a
30-100x bandwidth gap. Every technique below reduces *how much* data
must cross that gap, or hides the latency of crossing it -- none make
storage as fast as HBM.

== Layer 1: Physical NAND Media Bottlenecks

=== Read/Program/Erase Time Asymmetry `[PLAUSIBLE]`

NAND read, program, and erase operations are fundamentally asymmetric:

[cols="1,1,2", options="header"]
|===
| Operation | Typical latency | Note

| Page read (tR)
| ~40-110 μs
| TLC/QLC; SLC is faster (tens of μs)

| Page program (tPROG)
| ~600-2,400 μs
| 6-25x slower than a read

| Block erase (tBERS)
| ~3.5-12 ms
| 30-100x slower than a read
|===

These ranges are directionally consistent with published NAND
datasheet behavior (SLC devices are commonly cited around
35 μs / 350 μs / 1.5 ms for read/program/erase), but no primary
datasheet was cited for the TLC/QLC figures above -- treat exact
bounds as estimates, not verified specification values.

*Impact:* When a distributed training checkpoint (e.g., Megatron-LM /
DeepSpeed 3D parallelism) flushes hundreds of GB of optimizer state and
weights simultaneously, the drive's volatile write cache saturates and
collapses to native NAND program speed, stalling GPU clusters mid-run.

*Training impact:* CRITICAL -- multi-second cluster pauses at checkpoint commit. +
*Inference impact:* LOW under normal reads, but relevant to dynamic KV-cache spill writes.

=== Erase-Block vs. Page-Level Granularity Mismatch `[PLAUSIBLE]`

Flash is written in pages (16-32 KB) but erased in whole blocks
(32-96 MB). Continuous small, unaligned updates -- token activation
caching, transient KV-cache paging, RL logging -- fragment blocks
immediately and force aggressive background compaction.

=== Read Disturb and Voltage Drift `[PLAUSIBLE]`

Reading a 3D QLC cell applies pass-voltages to adjacent unselected
wordlines. Repeated epoch traversal of static training datasets injects
weak charge into inactive cells, causing threshold-voltage drift. The
SSD must run background read-scrubbing and relocation, producing
sudden P99.9 tail-latency spikes that desynchronize parallel data
loaders.

*Training impact:* HIGH -- data-loader jitter across epochs. +
*Inference impact:* HIGH -- unpredictable latency spikes on high-concurrency weight reads.

=== Thermal Saturation `[PLAUSIBLE]`

Sustained PCIe Gen5/6 streaming (14-28 GB/s) raises controller, PMIC,
and NAND junction temperature past typical 70-85°C limits. Enterprise
form factors (E1.S, E3.S, U.2) can throttle channel frequency by
40-60% mid-training under sustained ingestion.

== Layer 2: Firmware / Flash Translation Layer (FTL) Bottlenecks

=== Unscheduled Garbage Collection (GC) `[PLAUSIBLE]` -- highest-severity item in this document

GC reads valid pages from a fragmented block, rewrites them to a free
block, then erases the original. Standard FTLs cannot predict host
scheduling, so GC routinely collides with critical host reads. In
distributed training governed by synchronous collective ops
(`AllReduce`, `AllToAll`), a single SSD taking a 10 ms GC pause stalls
the *entire cluster* at the next gradient-sync barrier -- the
"straggler problem."

*Training impact:* CRITICAL -- cluster-wide stall from one drive. +
*Inference impact:* CRITICAL -- degrades P99.9 time-to-first-token (TTFT).

This is arguably the sharpest, most specific insight across all three
source documents: GC-induced tail latency isn't just "slower reads,"
it's a synchronization-barrier stall that scales to the whole cluster.

=== Semantic Blindness and Write Amplification `[PLAUSIBLE]`

A standard FTL treats all writes as one homogeneous byte stream. It
cannot distinguish:

* Immutable base weights (read-only, long-lived)
* Optimizer states (large sequential blocks, overwritten per epoch/checkpoint)
* Intermediate activations / KV tokens (transient, sub-second to multi-second lifetime)

Co-locating these different-lifetime streams in shared physical blocks
causes internal fragmentation, pushing Write Amplification Factor
(WAF) from an ideal ~1.05x up to an estimated 3.5-5.0x, roughly halving
sustainable sequential write bandwidth and degrading NAND cycle life.
The exact multiplier is an estimate, not a cited measurement.

=== FTL DRAM Mapping-Table Scaling `[PLAUSIBLE]`

Page-level address translation costs roughly 1 GB of internal DRAM per
1 TB of raw NAND (0.1% ratio, 4 KB granularity) -- a widely-cited
industry rule of thumb. For emerging 61-245 TB enterprise AI SSDs, that
implies 64-256 GB of onboard DRAM, which is cost-prohibitive,
power-hungry, and thermally constrained. Fallback mechanisms (demand-
paged FTL, Host Memory Buffer) add PCIe round-trips for address
lookups, adding an estimated 15-30 μs per access.

*Training impact:* HIGH -- high drive cost or slow paging for 60+ TB training lakes. +
*Inference impact:* HIGH -- multi-hop translation increases random embedding-retrieval latency.

=== Mapping Overhead, General `[SOURCED, general concern]`

Large AI datasets create substantial mapping-table and metadata
activity independent of the above capacity-scaling issue: lookup
latency, metadata traffic, and mapping updates all add overhead that
a workload-aware mapping cache or AI-aware placement could reduce.

== Layer 3: Controller Architecture Bottlenecks

=== Crossbar Structural Hazards on Scatter-Gather `[PLAUSIBLE]`

Controllers use multi-channel configs (8/16/32 channels) via an
internal crossbar or Network-on-Chip, optimized for broad contiguous
bursts. RAG vector search and sparse embedding lookups (e.g., DLRM
models) generate millions of non-contiguous point lookups across
different dies, triggering head-of-line blocking and channel
starvation.

*Training impact:* MODERATE -- reduced preprocessing throughput for multi-modal batches. +
*Inference impact:* CRITICAL -- head-of-line blocking on parallel RAG vector-index traversal.

=== Embedded Processor Compute Deficit `[PLAUSIBLE]`

SSD controllers run low-power embedded cores (Arm Cortex-R8/R52,
RISC-V) built purely for protocol compliance, wear leveling, and
timing management -- no SIMD, no hardware float, no high-throughput
memory bandwidth. The drive cannot autonomously decompress
(`zstd`/`snappy`), unpack columnar formats (Parquet/Arrow), or filter
tokens on-drive without added silicon.

This is the practical constraint underneath nearly every "computational
storage" proposal in this document: the compute has to come from
somewhere, and today's controller cores don't have it.

=== NAND Parallelism Underutilization `[SOURCED, general concern]`

Theoretical channel/die/plane bandwidth is only realized with enough
independent in-flight requests. AI workloads with skewed or bursty
access patterns can leave some channels idle while others queue.

=== Soft-Decision LDPC Error-Correction Walls `[PLAUSIBLE]`

As P/E cycling raises raw bit error rate, the ECC engine falls back
from single-cycle hard-decision LDPC to multi-iteration soft-decision
decoding with repeated fine-grained voltage sensing -- an estimated
jump from ~5 μs to 120+ μs per page, causing batch-processing jitter.

== Layer 4: Interconnect / Protocol / Host Boundary Bottlenecks

=== The PCIe "Data Tax" `[SOURCED numbers, PLAUSIBLE framing]`

Every sample, checkpoint, and embedding must cross PCIe traces, host
switches, CPU root complex, and system DRAM before reaching GPU HBM.
PCIe Gen5 (~64 GB/s) versus HBM3e (3.2-4.8 TB/s) is a two-order-of-
magnitude gap, and moving raw uncurated data across it costs real
dynamic energy (estimated 10-14 W per active PCIe link) and saturates
the host memory bus.

=== Block Abstraction vs. Tensor Granularity `[PLAUSIBLE, math is exact]`

NVMe is a block protocol operating in 4 KB sector multiples. Deep
learning kernels operate on individual scalars (FP16/BF16 = 2 bytes,
FP8 = 1 byte). Fetching a single 8-byte vector feature or one KV
attention head still requires pulling the full 4 KB block --
4096 / 8 = *512x* overfetch. This specific multiplier is simple
arithmetic given the stated block/feature sizes, not an external
citation, but it follows directly and correctly from those sizes.

*Inference impact:* CRITICAL -- this is likely the single biggest
structural mismatch for fine-grained inference access patterns
(isolated tokens, single attention heads, individual embedding rows).

=== CPU-Memory Bounce Buffers `[SOURCED]`

Traditional path: `SSD -> CPU memory (pinned buffer) -> kernel copy -> GPU memory`.
GPUDirect Storage (GDS) collapses this to `GPU HBM -> PCIe -> NVMe controller`
directly, removing the CPU, pinned buffer, and kernel copy.

CAUTION: GDS only helps when the pipeline is bandwidth-bound at the
CPU staging step. Compute-bound jobs not waiting on I/O see no benefit.
It is not a universal fix -- this is a real, measured, but
workload-dependent gain, not a blanket improvement.

=== OS / VFS Software Intermediation `[PLAUSIBLE]`

Legacy POSIX I/O involves multiple page copies, interrupt servicing,
VFS lock contention, and user-to-kernel context switches. Even with
GDS, metadata queries, layout resolution, and non-DMA fallback paths
add tens of microseconds -- enough to matter for real-time inference
latency budgets, even if negligible for bulk training throughput.

== Layer 5: System / Software-Architecture Bottlenecks

These sit above the individual drive and are not fixable by SSD
firmware alone, but they gate whether any SSD-level improvement
actually reaches the GPU.

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Bottleneck | Note

| Metadata operations at scale
| `[SOURCED]` As clusters grow, metadata hotspots and noisy neighbors leave GPUs waiting on I/O even when per-drive throughput is fine -- a distributed-systems problem, not a single-SSD problem.

| Object storage vs. file/block mismatch
| `[SOURCED]` The 2026 shift from episodic training to continuous, distributed inference is exposing limits of traditional file-based storage architectures at scale.

| Cost / tiering pressure
| `[SOURCED]` Flash-heavy storage, long retention, and multiple environment copies inflate spend faster than the AI roadmap grows, often forcing workloads onto slower, cheaper tiers -- an economic bottleneck that reintroduces latency indirectly.

| Multi-tenant / multi-GPU contention
| `[PLAUSIBLE]` Shared drives across tenants or GPUs need QoS, isolation, and fairness policies; without them, one noisy workload degrades another's tail latency.

| Data duplication
| `[PLAUSIBLE]` Data replicated across object storage, caches, CPU memory, SSD, and GPU memory wastes capacity, bandwidth, and energy redundantly.

| Networked storage / NVMe-oF
| `[PLAUSIBLE]` In distributed setups the path becomes GPU -> NIC/DPU -> network -> NVMe-oF -> SSD; network bandwidth and tail latency can dominate over local NAND performance.
|===

== Training-Specific Summary

Training workloads emphasize: sustained dataset throughput, batch
delivery, prefetch accuracy, CPU preprocessing, GPU starvation,
checkpoint I/O, large sequential/shuffled reads, distributed-sync
barriers, write amplification, endurance, and multi-GPU contention.

Representative path:

[source,text]
----
Dataset -> SSD -> Read/Prefetch -> CPU/DRAM -> Decode/Transform
        -> GPU Memory -> Training -> Checkpoint -> SSD
----

Most consequential bottlenecks for training, ranked by how directly
they stall the whole cluster (not just one node):

. Unscheduled GC colliding with `AllReduce`/`AllToAll` sync barriers `[PLAUSIBLE, CRITICAL]`
. Checkpoint write bursts exceeding write-cache absorption `[PLAUSIBLE/SOURCED, CRITICAL]`
. Random-read penalty during preprocessing (shuffle/tokenize/augment) `[SOURCED, HIGH]`
. Write amplification from semantic-blind FTL degrading sustained throughput `[PLAUSIBLE, HIGH]`
. Host-hop CPU/kernel-copy tax, partially solved by GDS for bandwidth-bound stages `[SOURCED, workload-dependent]`

== Inference-Specific Summary

Inference emphasizes: time-to-first-token, token-generation latency,
P99/P99.9 tail latency, model loading, embedding access, KV-cache
capacity/movement, multi-request contention, power efficiency, and
cold-start behavior.

Representative path:

[source,text]
----
Request -> Tokenizer -> Model/Weights -> KV Cache -> GPU -> Generated Tokens
----

Most consequential bottlenecks for inference:

. Block-vs-tensor overfetch (512x) on fine-grained lookups `[PLAUSIBLE arithmetic, CRITICAL]`
. GC-induced P99.9 TTFT degradation `[PLAUSIBLE, CRITICAL]`
. Crossbar head-of-line blocking on RAG vector-index traversal `[PLAUSIBLE, CRITICAL]`
. KV-cache overflow forcing spillover to slower tiers `[SOURCED problem / SPECULATIVE fix]`
. Cold-start / model-loading latency for large or infrequently-used weight files `[SOURCED]`

== Proposed Directions -- and How Solid Each One Is

[cols="3,2,4", options="header"]
|===
| Direction | Confidence | Note

| GPUDirect Storage / direct DMA paths
| `[SOURCED, shipping]`
| Real, measured, but only helps bandwidth-bound (not compute-bound) stages.

| NVMe Flexible Data Placement (FDP) for lifetime-aware write isolation
| `[SOURCED, real NVMe standard]`
| Isolates weights/optimizer-state/KV-cache into separate physical arenas to reduce WAF; the standard exists, AI-specific tuning of it does not yet.

| Near-storage SIMD/tensor micro-engines (decompress, filter, quantize, vector-distance pruning)
| `[SPECULATIVE]`
| Conceptually sound given the controller compute-deficit problem above, but no production silicon does this at scale today -- research/prototype stage only (e.g., FPGA-based "SmartSSD"-class offload).

| Hybrid non-volatile caching (MRAM/FeRAM + pSLC buffers) to absorb checkpoint bursts
| `[SPECULATIVE]`
| Reasonable direction, unproven at the capacities/costs needed for multi-hundred-GB checkpoint bursts.

| CXL.mem / CXL.io byte-addressable interconnects
| `[SOURCED technology, SPECULATIVE for this use case]`
| CXL is real and shipping in some server platforms; its use to bridge tensor-register-to-block-persistence semantics for AI storage is still conceptual.

| AI-aware predictive cache (reuse-probability scoring beyond LRU/LFU)
| `[SPECULATIVE]`
| Reasonable given known AI access structure (epoch repetition, batch boundaries, embedding reuse), but no evidence of a shipping implementation.

| GPU-aware / workload-aware SSD firmware scheduler (QoS for accelerator-critical I/O)
| `[SPECULATIVE]`
| Extends existing QoS concepts from enterprise storage; AI-specific version not yet demonstrated.

| On-drive vector search / embedding gather engines
| `[SPECULATIVE]`
| Directly addresses the crossbar and overfetch problems above, but is the least proven concept in this document.
|===

== Central Open Research Question

____
Can an AI-workload-aware SSD firmware reduce CPU, DRAM, and PCIe data
movement by jointly performing predictive caching, prefetching, and
selected computational preprocessing close to storage -- while
maintaining NAND-level efficiency, QoS, endurance, and thermal
constraints?
____

A credible proposal in this space should not just claim "the SSD gets
faster." It should show the SSD makes the *overall system* more
efficient, by deciding: what to cache, what to prefetch, what to
bypass, what computation to run near storage, when background
operations should run, which accelerator request gets priority, how
many transferred bytes can be avoided, and how much energy/wear is
saved without breaching latency limits.

== Recommended Evaluation Method

Compare incrementally rather than only baseline-vs-final-design:

[source,text]
----
Baseline SSD
   -> + AI-aware Cache
       -> + Predictive Prefetch
           -> + Computational Storage
               -> Full AI-aware SSD
----

Key metrics: GPU utilization, training samples/sec, inference latency
(mean and P99/P99.9), CPU utilization, DRAM bandwidth, PCIe traffic,
SSD bandwidth, cache hit rate, prefetch accuracy, energy per
sample/token, write amplification, NAND endurance, thermal behavior.

== Honest Overall Assessment

* The **physics-layer bottlenecks** (NAND asymmetry, GC stalls, block/tensor
  granularity mismatch) are the most severe and the least fixable by
  software alone -- they are hard physical/protocol constraints.
* The **firmware-layer bottlenecks** (semantic-blind FTL, WAF, DRAM
  scaling) are real and addressable with foreseeable engineering, but
  current published figures on their magnitude should be treated as
  estimates, not verified measurements, absent primary sources.
* The **system-layer bottlenecks** (metadata at scale, tiering economics,
  object-storage limits) are real today but are distributed-systems and
  software-architecture problems -- no SSD redesign fixes them alone.
* Every proposed *fix* tagged `[SPECULATIVE]` above is conceptually
  reasonable given the bottleneck it targets, but none has demonstrated
  production-scale deployment as of this writing. Treat this section as
  a research agenda, not a list of available solutions.
